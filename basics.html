Level 1 of 6 · about 2 minutes

What is annotation, and why bother?

STAKES: FOUNDATION Difficulty: 1 out of 3

Annotation means writing your own short notes on a text as you read it—in the margin, as a comment in a doc, or on a sticky note. You're not copying the text. You're adding comments as you read, showing what you notice, question, connect with, or want to investigate further. Look at this exatract from a study skills textbook:

"Spaced repetition means spreading your study sessions out over days or weeks, rather than cramming everything the night before."

A good annotation next to that line might say:

🔑 "spaced repetition = study a bit, often"

🔗 "This reminds me of building fitness: short, regular practice works better than one long session."

Annotating shows your marker you're actually thinking about the material, not just reading over it. Every annotation should show your thinking: a question, a link, a reflection, or a note to check something later.

The learning point

Only one of these shows genuine thinking. The rest either repeat the text, react with no explanation, or are not clearly in your own words. Level 4 digs into descriptive vs analytical annotations in more depth.

The learning point

A sentence starter only works if you finish it with something specific and relevant. If you write “I wonder… not sure”, add what you are unsure about. If you write “This reminds me of…”, name the specific experience, idea, or example it connects to.

The rule of thumb

An analytical annotation shows your thinking: a real question, a specific link, or a genuine reflection. A descriptive one just restates the text or reacts with no clear explanation, so it may not prove you understood the material.

Level 5 of 6 · about 3 minutes

Annotate a whole paragraph

STAKES: APPLY IT Difficulty: 3 out of 3

Worked example: four annotations on one paragraph

Short paragraph:

"Students often feel that re-reading their notes is a useful study strategy because it feels familiar and comfortable. However, research on learning suggests that methods requiring effort, such as self-testing, spaced practice, and explaining ideas in your own words, usually lead to stronger long-term understanding."
  • KEY TERM Self-testing means trying to answer questions from memory before checking the notes.
  • CONNECTION This links to how I remember practical steps better when I practise them without looking at instructions first.
  • REFLECTION This matters because I often choose the study method that feels easiest, not the one that helps me remember more later.
  • INVESTIGATE I need to check what “spaced practice” looks like for this course — daily review, weekly review, or both?

Why this works

The annotations are short, varied, and linked to specific parts of the paragraph. They do more than repeat the text: they define, connect, reflect, and identify something to check.

The learning point

A good annotation set is chosen carefully, written in your own words, varied in type, tracks your sources, and is readable by someone else. If your marker can easily follow your analytical thinking, you've done it right.
